Prove that for every positive integer $n$,

$$
1 + 2 + \cdots + n \;=\; \frac{n(n+1)}{2}.
$$

State your hypotheses clearly and use mathematical induction.

---SOLUTION---

**Claim.** For every positive integer $n$, $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n} k = \frac{n(n+1)}{2}$.

**Proof.** By induction on $n$.

*Base case* ($n=1$): $\sum_{k=1}^{1} k = 1$ and $\frac{1 \cdot 2}{2} = 1$, so
the identity holds.

*Inductive step.* Assume the identity holds for some $n \geq 1$, i.e.
$\sum_{k=1}^{n} k = \frac{n(n+1)}{2}$ (the inductive hypothesis). Then

$$
\sum_{k=1}^{n+1} k \;=\; \left(\sum_{k=1}^{n} k\right) + (n+1)
\;=\; \frac{n(n+1)}{2} + (n+1)
\;=\; \frac{n(n+1) + 2(n+1)}{2}
\;=\; \frac{(n+1)(n+2)}{2}.
$$

This is exactly the identity at $n+1$. By induction, the claim holds for all
positive integers $n$. $\square$
